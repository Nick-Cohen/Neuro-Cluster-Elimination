# 2026-W33 — Weekly Summary

> Three days of pure correctness work, and it was worth it. The week found and fixed **four
> independent bugs** that had been silently shaping results — a non-determinism that made $\log Z$
> unreproducible at a fixed seed, a frozen per-cluster sample count confounded with the
> experimental arm, a broken backward-factor path under [[bucket-merging]], and a redundant NN
> evaluation costing up to 25× on the dominant code path — then measured the damage to the
> published study (**mostly honest**, but the time-optimal $e_{\max}$ moved) and built the
> bit-exact regression suite the project had never been able to write. In parallel,
> [[wmb-residual-learning]] went from a pilot to a result with a mechanism, an epoch policy, a
> cost fix and an adversarial control. **Nothing merged to `main`.**

## Findings

### Correctness

- **Runs were not reproducible at a fixed seed, and the cause was `set()` over identity-hashed
  objects** — `FastFactor` defines no `__hash__`/`__eq__`, so bucket factors were ordered by memory
  address, perturbing targets by 3–4 float32 ULP and diverging through SGD into a **0.44 $\log Z$
  spread**. Fixed; post-fix replication is bit-exact at zero wall-clock cost. Notably, early
  stopping is **measured not to be the amplifier** — removing it makes the spread *worse*.
  → [[bit-exact-reproducibility]] (docs 09, 19, 21, 23, 28, 30)
- **The per-cluster sample count ran once and froze**, because the resolution wrote an int back
  into the shared run config. 57–94% of NN clusters were under-sampled, by up to 8×, **in different
  directions in different arms** — so it is confounded with the merge strategy, not just noisy.
  Structural results are unaffected. → [[num-samples-freeze]] (doc 11)
- **Backward-factor population was broken under merging** — crashing under two of four merge
  strategies, and where it ran, handing merged clusters a single-variable surrogate for their
  upstream that was off by **2.699 $\log_{10}$** on one cluster. Repaired. →
  [[backward-factor-population-under-merging]] (docs 01, 02, 10)
- **The NN path enumerated elimination variables the network does not contain**, evaluating
  15.8M rows where 1.1M distinct exist (14.2×) — the table path had always done it correctly. It is
  a *merging* bug: impossible at `nomerge`, 1.01–1.07× under subsumption, 1.25–4.06× under reduce-NN.
  → [[nn-elim-enumeration-redundancy]] (docs 03, 06, 09)
- **`compute_wmb_message` crashed on incoming NN messages**, firing on the second NN cluster of any
  real elimination — which is why earlier probes silently ran with exact upstream context. Fixed,
  recorded in the [[weighted-mini-bucket]] gotcha section. (docs 14, 16)
- **The project had no usable convergence diagnostic**: `Trainer.val_losses` was write-only, and
  the training loss underflows to exactly 0.0 on 82–99% of epochs under `neurobe_weighted_mse` at
  float32. → [[convergence-diagnostic-gap]] (docs 16, 18, 19)

### Damage assessment

- **The June study's error bars were mostly honest.** Artefact share of $\log Z$ variance is 1/2/5%
  on grid cells and 31% on one RBM cell; seed variance dominates in all four. Re-running would
  tighten intervals, not rewrite conclusions — **but gaps below ~0.24 $\log Z$ were never decided**
  (doc 23).
- **The time-optimal $e_{\max}$ moved, and further than predicted**: grid40x40.f15 from 6 to **≥16**
  (unbracketed), grid20x20.f5 from 10 to **14**, grid10x10 wrap unchanged (a clean null control).
  Two forces push it up from opposite ends — generation collapsed, *and* training inflated most at
  low $e_{\max}$. → [[time-optimal-merge-bound]] (docs 13, 15, 17)
- **Sample generation is not what caps $e_{\max}$** (doc 13). The corrected marginal $\gamma$ is
  **19× smaller** than published, because ~60% of the published value was intercept and the
  post-fix per-factor NN:table ratio is 8.1×, not 400–800×. Fitted cost model $R^2$ = 0.9960 on 330
  rows, independently confirmed twice.
- **Published summary statistics do not all reconcile** (doc 27). The median optimal $e_{\max}$ is
  8 (IQR [6,10]) over 27 cells, not the published 10 (IQR [6,12]) — which is internally impossible
  given the eight grid40 sixes. $\gamma$'s headline scalar comes from a different run than the CSV
  that shipped. Two of the four-arm cost-table rows are not reproducible on the population their
  header claims.

### Method: WMB residual learning

- **It works, and the mechanism is the output parameterisation, not variance reduction.** Arms 2,
  3 and 4 share an identical residual target; only how the net is asked to emit it changes, and
  that moves the result from 1.7× worse than baseline to **3.7× better**, monotonically. Doc 02's
  variance premise is **necessary-but-not-sufficient**, not false — it sets the size of the prize;
  conditioning decides whether you collect it. (docs 14, 16, 18, 20)
- **Its apparent cost was an Adam artefact.** The wrapper's output multiplier $s$ scales every
  gradient by $s^2$, which Adam is invariant to *except through `eps`* — 92% of coordinates ended
  training `eps`-dominated. Compensating brings 3.86× → **1.13× with zero accuracy traded**.
- **The adversarial control settles it**: tuning `eps` on the baseline recovers ~26% of the gain,
  itself indistinguishable from zero, while the residual parameterisation still wins with the `eps`
  regime held fixed on both sides. **`eps` is a cost knob; the parameterisation is an accuracy
  knob.** Side finding, actionable alone: `eps` = 1e-10 makes the NeuroBE baseline **0.75× cheaper
  at no accuracy cost**. → [[adam-eps-and-loss-scale]] (docs 22, 26)

## Experiments

- **γ v2 sweep** (doc 13): 41 runs / 656 rows / 24 min on gpu0, zero failures, a git commit on
  every row. Six passes including a held-out replication.
- **Post-fix $e_{\max}$ scan** (doc 17): 12 runs, gpu0+gpu3, one seed. Estimated 76 GPU-min, used
  **402** (5.3× over) because the estimate held the pre-fix training loop fixed; 9 of 21 planned
  runs were cut mid-flight, which is why grid40's optimum is a lower bound.
- **Error-bar decomposition** (doc 23): 32 runs, 48 GPU-min, gpu3.
- **Residual arms** (docs 14, 16, 18, 22, 26): ~170 runs across five arms and two grid cells,
  gpu1, strictly sequential after doc 19 established that parallel runs invalidate wall times.
- **Determinism** (docs 21, 23, 28, 30): CPU factorial + CUDA replication; 63.1 GPU-min against a
  2h cap for doc 28, 17.8 min of 90 for doc 30.
- **Structure-only CPU passes** were the week's best value: 261 structures / 158k clusters in
  ~11 min answered the `_get_slices` generality question (doc 06); 478 FastGM builds recovered the
  two predictors doc 04 needed; 116 (problem, merge) cells sized the residual sweep (doc 19).

## Blockers / decisions needed

- **Nothing is merged.** Seven branches, several with "check in the pre-existing uncommitted `nce/`
  working state" as their first commit — because doc 10 found that **no branch in the repository
  contained the merge passes, the tree-collect populator or `proposal_sampler.py`**. This is the
  same "no single source of truth" blocker raised in [[2026-W24]] on 2026-06-12, still open.
- **`e_max` semantics unresolved**: Nick's stated rule is a state-space product, the code caps a
  variable count, the fix is ~6–15 lines and was scoped twice without being applied. Docs 25 and 27
  disagree on whether it matters (four orders of magnitude vs quantitatively negligible). →
  [[terminology-map]]
- **Published numbers need a correction pass** before anything ships (doc 27): the median $e_{\max}$
  sentence, the γ provenance, and two cost-table rows.
- **`FastFactor` still has no `__hash__`/`__eq__`**, so the determinism bug's *class* is unfixed and
  there is no lint or CI rule against it.
- **Runtime estimates were wrong by 5.3× once**, because they held a fixed cost that a landed fix
  had changed. Any estimate crossing a code-version boundary needs re-derivation, not scaling.
